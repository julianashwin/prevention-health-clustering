The K = 3 AR(1) + transitory classes on theta with the cost curve estimated in pounds on theta (a degree-3 polynomial on the cost index, waves 7-15). Savings are exact: the cost curve is a cubic, so the expansion in mu stops at the cube. (a) The treatment cost avoided by prevention at mu = 0.1 (a tenth of each class's future decline held back) from start age r, by class, line width proportional to the class share, dashed the uniform policy. (b) C(r), the expected treatment cost from r to 90 on the observed cross-section, split into the cost at the mean path, what the class differences add and what realised deviations add (the part prevention cannot reach), with the saving from uniform prevention at mu = 1 (the whole decline held back). (c) The saving at mu = 0.1 in each year of age a from prevention started at 25 (solid) and 60 (dashed), by class, the per-age terms whose sum is (a). Pounds to 90, no survival weighting, no discounting. (d) Targeting at mu = 0.1, on the same classes refitted with each person's first two rows held out (29,899 people): each rule predicts a person's saving from what is known at t-1, the later of the two held rows (t is the first fitted row) (a multinomial logit of the class on a quadratic in age plus the set shown, fitted on a 70% split), treats the top q of the other 30% by predicted saving and is scored by the realised saving, the fitted-window class posterior times S_j(r; 0.1) at the person's age at t-1. Dashed, targeting on the estimated class, the posterior from the whole fitted window; dotted, everyone treated. The other rules and the share-of-return view are in the appendix targeting figure.
