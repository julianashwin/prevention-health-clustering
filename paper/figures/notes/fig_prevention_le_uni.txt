The univariate K = 3 AR(1) + transitory classes on theta (38,963 people), class 1 worst health, with Gompertz-Makeham class hazards estimated by posterior-weighted maximum likelihood on the multidim contract's death intervals (2,045 deaths) and calibrated to the ONS life tables (one common age-varying factor on every class hazard, matching the population LE at 65 and 90). Prevention from r at intensity mu holds back a share mu of the class's mean change in theta after r - 1 (the paper's instrument; the gain is held at its value at 90 beyond). Mortality responds through the within-age gradient of death before the next wave in theta (a logit on theta, a quadratic in age and theta x age over 363,033 person-waves at risk, 1,825 deaths: log-odds -0.99 per unit of theta at 55); the healthy share (general health good or better) through the same design fitted to the healthy indicator, applied to each class's person-waves at each age. Policies: targeted, class 1 at mu = 1 (its mean health held at the level of r - 1); uniform with the same budget, everyone at mu = pi_1(r) (class 1's share at r, 0.23 at 50); uniform at mu = 1. (a), (b) dashed: each class treated on its own at mu = 1. (c) class 3 minus class 1. (d) the standard deviation of age at death over the composition-weighted mixture. (e)-(g) each group's LE and HLE at 50 is its posterior class composition among the person-waves within five years of 50 times the class values, so the gradients are the part the types carry (within-class differences by group are not modelled); the label gives the group's worst-class share; regions sorted by baseline LE. (h) LE of the top minus the bottom group: degree or higher minus other/none, top minus bottom income third, and the range across the twelve regions. The within-age mortality gradient is at or below the one the estimated class hazards imply between types before 55 and above it after (-0.9 against -0.7 per unit at 70), so the late-life years are if anything generous.
